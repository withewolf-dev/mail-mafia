# The offer — Armstrong

The fixed half of every email. Prospect evidence supplies the other half; this
file supplies what we do about it, and what we are not allowed to say.

Read this as constraint, not as copy. Nothing here should appear in an email
verbatim except where marked. The failure mode this file exists to prevent is
an email padded with company language instead of findings about the recipient.

---

## What we do, in one sentence

> When someone asks an AI assistant where to go for something local, we make
> sure the business is one of the ones it names.

Never describe it as SEO, digital marketing, traffic, or "AI-powered" anything.
Never use the term GEO before explaining the behaviour.

## Why now — the behaviour, not the technology

> People increasingly ask ChatGPT, Gemini or Perplexity "who's good for this
> near me" and act on the answer, instead of scrolling results themselves.

Name the assistants. "AI models", "LLMs", "generative engines" are our
vocabulary and read as automation to a business owner.

---

## The deliverable: the AI Visibility Report

This is what the CTA promises, so it has to exist before any batch goes out.
For each of 4–6 high-intent queries in their city:

1. **The query** — "Where should I go for hair restoration in Ocala, Florida?"
2. **The sub-searches the assistant actually issued** — it rewrites the question
   before searching, e.g. `hair restoration hair transplant Ocala Florida`
3. **Every page it read** — typically 8–12 URLs per query
4. **Who it recommended**, in order
5. **Whether their own site appeared** in the pages read, across k runs

Then the pattern across all of it: which pages win, and what those pages have
that theirs doesn't.

**Why it lands:** it is not an opinion. It is a transcript of a machine
deciding, with their business absent from it.

## What we can prove today

- We ran the queries. Counts are real (`named in 0 of 3 runs`).
- We captured every source URL the assistant read.
- We know which competitors were named, by name.
- We can see structural differences between winning pages and theirs — for
  example rivals publishing at `/ocala/vaginal-rejuvenation/` while the
  prospect's equivalent page never states the city.

## Never claim

Not because they're impolite — because they're unverifiable, and the recipient
can check.

- Lost revenue, lost customers, lost bookings *as a quantity*
- Rankings, traffic numbers, or search volume we haven't measured
- That we tested ChatGPT specifically, unless we ran ChatGPT specifically
- Guaranteed placement, timelines, or "we'll get you to number one"
- Anything about their competitors beyond what a probe returned

Safe phrasing that says the same thing: *"which competitor is getting the
booking instead"* — backed by names we actually hold.

## After they say yes

> **FILL THIS IN.** I don't know Armstrong's pricing, engagement shape, or
> delivery timeline, and inventing them here would put invented terms into
> emails. Needed:
>
> - What the report costs, if anything (currently pitched as free)
> - What the paid engagement is, and roughly what it costs
> - How long the work takes
> - What the client has to provide (site access? CMS login?)
> - What "done" looks like — re-probe and show movement?

Until this section is real, the CTA must stop at offering the report. Do not
let an email imply a paid engagement whose terms nobody has written down.

## Objections we will get

Answers here feed the inbox lane's `OBJECTION` route. Each needs a real reply
before the first batch, not after.

| Objection | Honest answer |
|---|---|
| "We already do SEO." | Different surface. Ranking on Google and being named in an assistant's answer are decided by different signals; we can show their own queries either way. |
| "Does this actually drive business?" | Be straight: this is early, and the honest pitch is the transcript — their absence from answers people act on. Do not invent attribution data. |
| "How is this different from an agency?" | **FILL IN.** |
| "Isn't AI search still small?" | Don't oversell the number. The observable fact is that their category is being answered by assistants today, and they are not in the answers. |
| "Can you guarantee results?" | No. Say no. |

## Tone

A smart operator writing one note. Short sentences. Concrete over clever.
Numbers, names, quotes. An observation always beats a compliment.

Banned regardless of context: *hope you're doing well, wanted to reach out,
came across your, leverage, synergy, revolutionary, game-changing,
cutting-edge, unlock, skyrocket, 10x, guaranteed, dominate, elevate, transform,
boosting, quick question, growth hack.*
